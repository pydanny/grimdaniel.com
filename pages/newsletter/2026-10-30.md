Happy Halloween! In this 19th edition of the **Not Dead Yet Newsletter**, I talk about writing combat in stories, Halloween in an Asian country, and more.

## Combat in stories

In a story, a sword fight can be technically detailed, full of elaborate choreography, and still leave the reader completely confused. I've done a lot of training in weapons martial arts, specifically fencing, Kendo, Hima, and Eskrima, all of which have some form of full contact sparring. That gives me a lot of material and background and knowledge that I can draw on. It also gives me lots of opportunities to get in my own way. Because knowing something about the mechanics of weapons, distance management, and timing is very different than writing about it.

There's been so many times I wrote overly detailed fight scenes only to have beta readers tell me they got lost in the morass of details. It's a bitter pill to swallow.

The first thing that's easy for me to forget is that combat and fiction serves a story. Every time weapons are used, it should accomplish something. The problem is when I turn an element of a story into a lesson on melee fighting.

It gets worse when I add jargon. For example, take the sentence:

> "He executed an esquive, broke measure, and beat a hasty retreat."

Most readers won't know what this means. This works better:

> "He jumped back, turned tail, and ran."

This tells the reader in much clearer terms that the person is scared and trying to get out of the fight.

So when writing combat, I try to focus on economy of words rather than detail. Except for rare occasions I keep jargon out. I focus on plot and emotion, making the scene serve the plot.

## Halloween in Asia

Our seven year old daughter, Uma, loves holidays, especially Halloween! Fortunately, where we live (Manila, Philippines) starting last week there's been one kid-oriented Halloween event after another. Some highlights:

- Her witch costume won a prize at a Halloween party
- Trick-or-treating in a village with dozens of houses handing out candy
- Going for soup dumplings (Xaio lung bao) after trick-or-treating
- More trick-or-treating, this time between shops in a mall

She scored so much candy she decided to give most of it to our building's staff.
